CALIPER
Empirical Measurement of Container Confinement

Project Proposal
B.Tech, Computer Science and Engineering, Final Year
Four-member team


1. OVERVIEW

A container's security posture is declared in a manifest and enforced by the
kernel. These are not the same thing. The manifest names a seccomp profile,
a capability set and a Pod Security Standard; what the kernel actually
permits depends additionally on the container runtime, its version, the
node's distribution and loaded modules, the kernel build, the processor
architecture, the active Linux Security Module and the RuntimeClass. Two
clusters running an identical manifest can expose materially different
kernel attack surface, and nothing in the deployment pipeline reports the
difference.

Existing tooling addresses the two ends of this problem and not the middle.
Configuration scanners read the manifest and report what was declared.
Runtime monitors observe what the workload did. Neither answers the
question that sits between them: given this container, on this node, right
now, what can it actually reach in the kernel?

CALIPER is an instrument that answers that question by measurement. It runs
as an ordinary container, executes a corpus of argument-level reachability
probes against the kernel, records the exact verdict returned for each, and
emits a machine-readable confinement fingerprint keyed by the environment
that produced it. Fingerprints are diffed against one another, against the
declared manifest, and against a maintained reference posture, and the
divergences are reported with the minimal configuration change that closes
them.


2. MOTIVATION

The failure mode is documented and recent. CVE-2026-31431, tracked as Copy
Fail, is a logic flaw in the algif_aead module reached through the AF_ALG
address family. Combined with splice(2) it writes four bytes into the page
cache of any readable file, which is sufficient to modify a setuid binary in
memory without altering it on disk. CISA added it to the Known Exploited
Vulnerabilities catalogue on 1 May 2026.

Three properties of that vulnerability motivate this project.

First, it is reached through a socket address family, not through a
distinctive syscall. Filtering at syscall-name granularity cannot express
the distinction between a container that may call socket(2) and one that may
reach the kernel crypto API, and syscall-name granularity is what every
profile generator on the market emits.

Second, published testing across Talos and EKS found that the RuntimeDefault
seccomp profile in both containerd and Moby did not deny AF_ALG, and that
Pod Security Standards at the Restricted level did not prevent it either.
Workloads that satisfied every conventional hardening check remained
exploitable, and establishing this required hand-built test images and a
hand-authored profile.

Third, exploitability depends on whether algif_aead is loaded on the node,
which is a property of the distribution's module policy and not of anything
the workload author controls or can observe. The same manifest is exposed on
one node and not on another.

The general problem is that RuntimeDefault is runtime-defined. Its contents
are a property of the runtime binary on the node, not of the manifest, and
they change over time. io_uring, which defeats syscall filtering entirely by
performing operations without issuing the corresponding syscalls, was
removed from the default profile of Moby and containerd in November 2023,
and the change shipped in Moby 25.0 in January 2024 and in containerd 2.0
in November 2024 without a backport to containerd 1.7. AF_ALG itself
followed the same path in May 2026, when Moby added a deny for it in
29.4.2. A cluster whose node image predates those releases enforces a
weaker policy than an identically configured cluster whose node image does
not, and neither manifest records the distinction.

The declared posture is therefore an assertion, and it is currently
unverified. CALIPER exists to verify it.


3. RELATION TO EXISTING WORK

Work in this area falls into three categories, none of which measures
enforced confinement.

Configuration scanners, including kube-bench, Kubescape and Trivy, parse
manifests, Helm charts and cluster configuration against benchmarks. They
report what was declared. In the AF_ALG case the declaration was correct and
the enforcement was weaker than practitioners believed, and a configuration
scanner reports no finding.

Runtime monitors, including Falco, Tetragon and commercial equivalents,
observe syscalls as the workload issues them. They report what the workload
did, which is a subset of what it could have done. An attack surface that is
never exercised is invisible to them.

Profile generators, including oci-seccomp-bpf-hook, the Kubernetes Security
Profiles Operator, Inspektor Gadget and slimtoolkit, trace a workload and
emit a policy. They answer what the application needs, not what the
environment permits, and they emit policy at syscall-name granularity, which
is the granularity at which the AF_ALG case is invisible.

Two tools are direct ancestors. amicontained reports the container runtime,
namespaces, capabilities and a coarse list of blocked syscalls; it
established the idea, and its last release was v0.4.9 in November 2019 with
its last commit in September 2020. seccomp-diff extracts and disassembles
the seccomp-BPF filter from a running process and diffs two of them; it is
confined to seccomp, requires privilege that an unprivileged container does
not hold, and reasons about the filter rather than about reachability, so it
cannot account for capabilities, LSM policy, mount masking, loaded modules
or architecture.

Houdini (Patel et al., PST 2025) proposes benchmarking container security
confinement and is the closest published statement of the problem. It is a
research contribution and no tool was released.

CALIPER is an engineering contribution and is presented as such. Its claim
is not that the idea is new but that no instrument implementing it exists.


4. OBJECTIVES

    1.   To implement a probe engine that executes reachability probes under
         fork isolation, with bounded and accounted side effects, and
         classifies each outcome precisely, distinguishing permitted,
         denied, unimplemented and fatally filtered results.

    2.   To implement a probe corpus operating at argument granularity,
         covering, as the committed set, socket address families and
         netlink protocol numbers, io_uring opcode reach, mount filesystem
         types, clone and unshare flags, masked and read-only path
         coverage, device node access, and capability effect. A deferred
         set, comprising ioctl request codes, bpf commands and program
         types, keyctl operations, prctl options, perf_event_open,
         userfaultfd, open_by_handle_at, module loading and seccomp user
         notification, is specified in the same format and implemented as
         time permits.

    3.   To implement denial attribution based principally on errno oracles,
         identifying which enforcement mechanism produced a given denial,
         and reporting attribution confidence where the mechanism cannot be
         determined without privilege.

    4.   To define a stable confinement fingerprint format whose identity
         includes processor architecture, kernel version, distribution and
         loaded modules, runtime and runtime version, LSM and RuntimeClass,
         and a diff engine that reports divergence at probe granularity and
         classifies every divergence as architecture-explained,
         kernel-version-explained, runtime-class-explained or
         policy-explained.

    5.   To author and maintain a reference posture: a versioned, cited
         model asserting which kernel entry families a workload at a named
         security tier should not reach, against which measurements are
         evaluated.

    6.   To implement a reporting layer that evaluates a fingerprint against
         both the declared manifest and the reference posture, and
         distinguishes claim violations, expectation violations and
         undeclared exposure.

    7.   To implement a remediation generator that emits the minimal
         argument-level policy closing the divergences found, expressed both
         as a Localhost seccomp profile and as a Security Profiles Operator
         custom resource, and verifies closure by re-probing.

    8.   To evaluate the instrument across at least six distinct
         environment cells spanning architecture, runtime, runtime version,
         LSM and RuntimeClass, reporting measured divergence and
         reproducing the AF_ALG case unaided.


5. ARCHITECTURE

    Probe Engine            fork isolation, probe execution, verdict
                            classification, side effect accounting
        |
    Probe Corpus            argument-level probe definitions with their
                            attribution metadata, grouped by entry family
        |
    Attributor              errno oracles, capability and LSM
                            correlation, confidence scoring
        |
    Fingerprint and Diff    environment-keyed serialisation, divergence
                            reporting, divergence classification
        |
    Evaluator               manifest parsing, reference posture
                            evaluation, finding categorisation
        |
    Remediator              minimal policy emission, closure verification

The fingerprint format is the fixed interface of the system. Its first
version is agreed in week two and developed against thereafter, and it is
frozen at the end of week nine, after which any change is a new format
version. The engine and corpus produce the measurement, the control plane
completes it into a fingerprint, and the diff engine, evaluator and
remediator consume it. This allows the four workstreams to proceed without
blocking one another, and allows the reporting layers to be developed
against recorded fingerprints long before the corpus is complete.

The fingerprint's identity is the environment cell, not the workload.
Architecture, kernel version, distribution and loaded modules, runtime and
runtime version, LSM and RuntimeClass are recorded as first-class fields,
because a divergence between an arm64 and an amd64 measurement is not a
policy finding and must not be reported as one. The loaded module set is
recorded before any probe executes, for the reason given in 6.2.

The cell is assembled by the control plane, not by the probe. The probe
records what is observable from inside an unprivileged container: the
architecture, the kernel's own version string, the loaded module set, its
capability sets and security-module label, and the seccomp mode. Runtime,
runtime version, distribution and RuntimeClass are not observable from
inside, and are taken from the Kubernetes Node object and the pod's
RuntimeClass, or supplied by the operator for a run outside Kubernetes,
and each is recorded together with its source. Attribution is computed by
the control plane from the raw verdicts and the probe's declared metadata;
the engine stores no interpretation. A kernel version reported by a
sandbox, as gVisor reports a synthetic one, is recorded as the sandbox's
claim and the RuntimeClass field, not the version, drives classification.

RuntimeClass is handled in the same spirit. gVisor implements a subset of
the syscall interface and reports a synthetic kernel version, and Kata
boots a guest kernel of its own, so a verdict that differs between a runc
cell and a sandboxed cell is explained by the sandbox before any policy is
consulted. The classifier therefore carries a runtime-class-explained
class, assigned when the two cells differ in RuntimeClass and the
sandboxed side reports the entry point unimplemented; it is reported
beside the architecture class and never counted as policy. A difference
in LSM or in runtime version, by contrast, is a difference in the policy
the kernel actually enforces, and is classified as policy-explained.

The probe engine is implemented in Rust. The engine must fork before each
risky probe, must not issue syscalls of its own that pollute the
measurement, and must survive a probe terminated by SECCOMP_RET_KILL_PROCESS
without losing the run. A managed runtime that spawns threads and issues
background syscalls is unsuitable on all three counts. The control plane,
comprising fingerprint handling, diffing, evaluation, remediation and
Kubernetes and CI integration, is implemented in Go, where the container
ecosystem libraries are mature. The two halves meet at the fingerprint
format and nowhere else.


6. THE TWO HARD PROBLEMS

6.1 Attribution

A probe attempts an operation and receives EPERM. The kernel does not say
why. The denial may have come from the seccomp filter, from a dropped
capability, from AppArmor or SELinux, from an unmapped identity in a user
namespace, or from a read-only or masked mount. From inside the container
these are indistinguishable, and a report that cannot tell them apart cannot
produce a remediation, because the fix differs in each case.

The primary technique is the errno oracle. Each probe is authored with an
argument set chosen so that, if the syscall reaches the kernel at all, the
kernel is structurally guaranteed to return a specific and distinctive
error: EBADF for an operation on a deliberately invalid descriptor, EINVAL
for a malformed request, EAFNOSUPPORT for an address family outside the
kernel's range. Receiving EPERM where EBADF was guaranteed proves the call
was intercepted before the point at which the kernel would have produced
that error, and therefore that a filter and not a capability produced the
denial. The technique requires no privilege, works from inside any
container, and yields a positive proof rather than an inference. Its reach
is per syscall, and each oracle records it: descriptor resolution precedes
every security-module hook, so an EBADF oracle isolates seccomp, whereas
the hook for socket(2) runs before the family lookup, so an EAFNOSUPPORT
oracle on an in-range family separates a filter from a capability but not
from AppArmor or SELinux, which the label interfaces then decide. A filter
may also return ENOSYS rather than EPERM, as runc does for the syscalls it
stubs, and ENOSYS is treated as ambiguous rather than as unimplemented
whenever the cell's kernel is known to implement the syscall. Authoring
the oracle is part of authoring the probe, and the corpus is not
considered complete until every probe carries one.

Capability attribution is by correlation. The operation's documented
capability requirement is recorded with the probe and checked against the
effective and bounding sets read from the process's own status, which is
readable without privilege. Where a capability is absent and the errno
oracle indicates the call reached the kernel, the capability is the cause.

Security-module correlation is the third technique. The process's own
AppArmor or SELinux label is readable without privilege from its attr
directory under /proc, as is whether the module is enforcing. A denial
that the oracle places after the security-module hook, arriving as EACCES
under a confining label, is attributed to the module at reduced
confidence; under an unconfined label it is not, and the seccomp reading
stands.

Filter introspection is a fourth technique and its availability is limited.
PTRACE_SECCOMP_GET_FILTER requires CAP_SYS_ADMIN and a ptrace-attachable
target, which an unprivileged container does not have, and /proc/self/status
exposes only the seccomp mode and a count of installed filters, not their
contents. It is therefore available only to a privileged helper, which this
proposal outlines but does not specify, and which is not a committed
deliverable within the schedule. The design does not depend on it, and the
two techniques above need no privilege.

Some denials consequently remain undecidable from inside the container. The
design accepts this rather than concealing it. Attribution is reported with
a confidence level, and the residual undecidable fraction is reported as a
measured quantity rather than assumed to be zero. Reducing that fraction
through a privileged helper is the first extension the design admits.

6.2 Probe safety and survivability

An instrument that damages what it measures is not usable in the
environments where it is most needed. Every probe must be safe to run
against a production node.

Three constraints follow. Probes establish the reachability of a kernel
entry point and never its exploitation; the corpus contains no exploit code
and no operation whose success confers privilege. Where a subsystem offers
an enumeration interface it is preferred over execution: io_uring opcode
reach is measured with IORING_REGISTER_PROBE, which reports the supported
opcodes without submitting a single queue entry. Every probe declares its
side effects and its rollback, and probes with unbounded duration execute
under a timeout, with a probe that hangs recorded as such rather than
stalling the run.

Fork isolation is the rollback for most of the corpus. Every side effect
that is descriptor-shaped, which includes io_uring rings, BPF program and
map descriptors, sockets, userfaultfd and perf events, is reclaimed by the
kernel when the probe's child exits. Residual state is therefore measured
only for the classes that outlive a process, and is measured directly rather
than by whole-host comparison, which normal system churn would swamp: the
mount table, the session and user keyrings, cgroup entries, System V and
POSIX IPC objects, and network namespaces.

One side effect is neither descriptor-shaped nor reclaimable, and is
declared rather than rolled back. A socket(2) call on an address family the
kernel has not registered asks the kernel to load the module for it, as
does an unregistered netlink protocol number or a bind(2) to an algorithm
type on an AF_ALG socket, and the kernel does so with no capability check
on the caller. The probe that tests whether algif_aead is reachable can
therefore be the reason it is loaded, on a node that permits autoloading.
The engine records the loaded module set before any probe executes, so
that the environment cell reflects the node and not the instrument,
records it again after the run, and reports the difference as a finding
in its own right. The families that can trigger a load declare it, and the
reduced corpus of section 12 either omits them or runs them only when the
operator asks.

Survivability is the corresponding requirement. A probe that trips a filter
whose action is SECCOMP_RET_KILL_PROCESS terminates the process that issued
it, and an instrument that dies on its first genuine finding is worthless.
Each risky probe therefore executes in a forked child whose exit status,
including termination by SIGSYS, is itself the measurement. The common case
does not require this, since the default action of the RuntimeDefault
profile is SCMP_ACT_ERRNO rather than a kill; it is required for the
hand-authored hardened profiles that this instrument is most useful against,
and it is established in the first implementation phase because every
subsequent probe family depends on it.


7. THE REFERENCE POSTURE

The motivating example is not a claim violation. Pod Security Standards at
the Restricted level assert nothing about AF_ALG, and RuntimeDefault's
entire contract is that it contains whatever the runtime ships. Measured
against the manifest alone, the AF_ALG case produces no finding: the
declaration and the measurement agree, and both are weaker than
practitioners believe. A checker that compares measurement against the
manifest and nothing else would have missed the case this project exists to
detect.

What failed was an expectation, not a claim. Detecting it requires a model
of what a hardened workload ought not to reach, authored deliberately and
independently of what any specification happens to promise.

The reference posture is that model. It is a versioned document that, for
each named security tier, asserts which kernel entry families a workload at
that tier should not reach: for an untrusted multi-tenant tier, the kernel
crypto API, module loading, perf_event_open, raw netlink, io_uring, the
32-bit ABI, unmasked procfs, and the rest. Every assertion carries a
rationale and a citation, whether a CVE, a documented escape technique or a
vendor advisory, so that the posture is auditable rather than assertion by
authority. It is deliberately stronger than the tiers it is named after, and
that gap is the point of it.

The evaluator therefore produces three categories of finding.

    Expectation violation      reachable, and the reference posture holds
                               that it should not be. The AF_ALG case.

    Claim violation            the manifest asserts a protection that the
                               measurement does not confirm.

    Undeclared exposure        reachable, and addressed by neither model.
                               An input to corpus and posture development.

The reference posture also supplies the target that remediation is minimal
with respect to. Without it, a minimal patch has nothing to be minimal
against. It is released with the instrument and is, in practical terms, the
most reusable artefact the project produces.


8. WORK ALLOCATION

Four workstreams, cut along the fingerprint interface so that members are
not blocked on one another.

    Member A    Probe engine, safety and the attribution engine. Fork
                isolation, timeout handling, side effect declaration and
                accounting, verdict classification, and the probe
                specification format, including the specification of the
                deferred set. Delivers the engine in the first phase, then
                owns probe safety for the life of the project and takes
                half the corpus families. Also builds the errno oracle
                attribution engine: establishing that a call was
                intercepted before the kernel reached the point at which
                the oracle guaranteed its error is a property of the
                fork-and-verdict path, and belongs with the code that
                implements it rather than with the corpus that declares
                the oracles.

    Member B    Corpus, oracle discipline and reference posture. The
                remaining corpus families; the errno oracle discipline
                across the whole corpus, meaning the design of the first
                families' oracles, review of every oracle A authors with
                A's families, and the completeness check that gates the
                freeze; capability and security-module correlation; the
                confidence model and the measurement of the undecidable
                fraction; and the reference posture, whose assertions and
                citations follow the same entry families as the probes.
                Attribution is split rather than owned: B decides what a
                given errno proves, A builds the machinery that proves it.

    Member C    Fingerprint, diff and evaluation. Environment-keyed
                serialisation and versioning, the diff engine and its
                divergence classifier, manifest parsing, the evaluator, and
                reporting in JSON and human-readable form, with SARIF as an
                extension.

    Member D    Delivery and evaluation harness. Kubernetes Job and
                DaemonSet packaging, CI integration, the environment matrix
                and its automation, and the remediation generator with
                closure verification. Also acts as integrator, responsible
                for the components composing correctly.

The corpus is split between A and B rather than owned by one member,
because it is the largest single body of work and because attribution
metadata is authored with each probe rather than added afterwards. The
reference posture sits with B rather than C for the same reason: its
assertions are the entry families, and the citations are gathered while the
corresponding probes are written.

                    kernel-level and hard to predict.

    Week 17         Documentation. Final report, demonstration, release.

The system is independently defensible from the end of week 9, at which
point it measures, classifies and diffs confinement across environments
without evaluating it against a posture or remediating it. That is the
point below which the project should not be allowed to fall.

9.2 What the compression gives up

Four reductions make the schedule fit, and each is a deliberate choice
rather than an estimate.

The probe corpus is split into a committed set and a deferred set, as
Objective 2 records. The committed set is chosen to carry the motivating
case and the known filter-bypass paths; the deferred set is specified in
the same format so that it is additive work rather than redesign. Corpus
breadth is the safest thing to cut, because breadth is the substrate of
this project and the classifier, posture and evaluator are its
contribution.

The privileged helper is outlined but neither specified nor built.
Attribution therefore rests on errno oracles, capability correlation and the
process's own security-module label, none of which needs privilege, and the
undecidable fraction is reported rather than reduced.

The environment matrix is reduced to six required cells, with managed
clusters as additions, which also removes cloud account and budget risk
from the critical path.

SARIF output is deferred behind JSON and human-readable reporting.

Nothing in the measurement, classification or evaluation path is reduced.
The instrument does less, and reports what it does with the same rigour.

11. EVALUATION

11.1 Environment matrix

The unit of evaluation is the environment cell, defined by the tuple of
architecture, runtime and version, kernel, distribution and loaded modules,
LSM, and RuntimeClass. Cells are counted honestly: kind, minikube and k3s
on the same host are one cell, not three, because all three are containerd
on the same kernel. Docker Desktop and Colima on macOS, by contrast, run
different guest kernels and distributions and would be two cells, which is
why neither is used: cell 1 names its guest explicitly.

Six cells are required. Cell 2 is the baseline, and every other cell moves
as few axes as possible relative to it, so that a divergence is
attributable.

    1.  arm64, Ubuntu 24.04 under Lima, containerd 2.2 and runc, AppArmor
    2.  amd64, Ubuntu 24.04, containerd 2.2 and runc, AppArmor
    3.  amd64, Fedora or Rocky, containerd 2.2 and runc, SELinux
    4.  amd64, Ubuntu 24.04, containerd 1.7.12 and runc, AppArmor
    5.  amd64, Ubuntu 24.04, containerd 2.2, gVisor RuntimeClass
    6.  amd64, Ubuntu 24.04, containerd 2.2, Kata Containers RuntimeClass

Cells 4, 5 and 6 each move exactly one axis: runtime version, and
RuntimeClass twice. Cell 1 moves architecture and, since no distribution
builds an identical kernel for both, the kernel build with it; cell 3 moves
the LSM and the distribution together, since SELinux is not a supported
configuration of Ubuntu. Divergence in those two pairs is attributed to
architecture or to policy only after the kernel-version class has been
excluded probe by probe. The containerd versions are pinned rather than
left to the host's patch state: Ubuntu 24.04 ships 1.7.12 in its release
pocket and 2.2 in its updates pocket, and the io_uring deny entered the
default profile in 2.0, so an unpatched and a patched host of the same
release sit on either side of the change. Cell 4 also serves the AF_ALG
reproduction, whose RuntimeDefault profile must predate any AF_ALG deny.

Three further cells are additions, attempted if the schedule permits: an
amd64 Podman and crun cell and an amd64 Moby cell, both on the AppArmor
host, so that the runtime axis of objective 8 is moved rather than only the
runtime version; EKS on Amazon Linux 2023 or GKE on Container-Optimized OS;
and Talos, the platform on which the motivating result was published. All
six required cells run on developer hardware, which assumes that the team
has one amd64 machine with hardware virtualisation: the Kata cell needs it
outright, and the other amd64 cells run on an arm64 laptop only under
emulation, too slowly to measure runtime cost. That machine is confirmed in
week 1. With it, the evaluation depends on neither cloud budget nor account
availability, and managed clusters cannot delay the critical path.

11.2 Metrics and success criteria

    Divergence across cells, as the number of probes whose verdict differs
    between cell pairs, reported separately for each divergence class.
    Success: policy-explained divergence is measured in at least four cell
    pairs, and architecture-explained and runtime-class-explained divergence
    are correctly separated from it in every pair.

    Expectation and claim violations, by category and by cell. Success: at
    least one expectation violation found in at least three distinct cells.

    Reproduction of the AF_ALG case unaided, demonstrating detection of what
    previously required bespoke test images. Success: binary.

    Attribution accuracy against a constructed ground truth of environments
    in which each denial has a single known cause. Success: at least 90 per
    cent of the denials the instrument attributes are attributed correctly,
    and the fraction it reports as undecidable, measured over all denials,
    does not exceed 10 per cent.

    Remediation closure rate, verified by re-probing. Success: 100 per cent
    for seccomp-attributed divergences; reported without a threshold for
    divergences attributed to other mechanisms, which the instrument may
    identify but cannot always close.

    Residual state after a full run, across the five tracked object classes.
    Success: zero unreclaimed objects. Modules loaded by autoload are
    reported separately and are not counted, since the container cannot
    reclaim them.

    Runtime cost. Success: full corpus under 60 seconds, reduced CI corpus
    under 5 seconds.

    Comparison against amicontained and seccomp-diff on identical targets,
    reported as findings produced by each.


12. RISKS

    The reference posture is authored by us and is therefore a judgement.
    Mitigated by requiring a citation for every assertion, by versioning it
    so that findings are reproducible against a stated posture revision, and
    by reporting expectation violations separately from claim violations so
    that a reader can weigh them differently.

    Remediation may be undeliverable on managed clusters. A Localhost
    seccomp profile requires the file to exist at the kubelet's seccomp root
    on every node, which is not achievable on EKS or GKE without custom node
    images or a privileged installer. Mitigated by emitting a Security
    Profiles Operator custom resource as the primary output form, which
    solves distribution, and by stating that closure verification is
    performed on self-managed nodes.

    The instrument may be indistinguishable from an attack to runtime
    security tooling. Mitigated by publishing a documented signature for the
    probe image and by providing a reduced corpus that omits the probes most
    likely to raise alerts, so that operators can allowlist it deliberately.
    That corpus necessarily omits the AF_ALG probe, on which the published
    Copy Fail detection rules key; on a monitored cluster the motivating
    case is demonstrated by allowlisting the signed image and running the
    full corpus.

    Denial attribution may be partially undecidable. Addressed by the errno
    oracle as the primary technique, which needs no privilege, and by the
    confidence model, with the residual fraction reported as a finding. The
    instrument remains useful with attribution disabled, since reachability
    is the primary measurement and attribution refines it.

    The project may become breadth without depth, producing a larger
    amicontained rather than an instrument. This is the principal risk. It
    is mitigated by freezing the corpus at a declared size at the end of
    week 8 and by treating the diff classifier, the reference posture and
    the evaluator as the contribution, not the corpus.

    A probe may destabilise a host during development. Mitigated by
    development inside disposable virtual machines with snapshot rollback,
    and by the side effect declaration requirement, which the engine
    enforces rather than leaving to convention.

    Four-member projects fail through unclear ownership. Addressed by
    cutting along the fingerprint interface, with one member explicitly
    responsible for integration.

    The schedule is compressed and has correspondingly little tolerance
    for a foundational slip. The first three weeks are the exposure: every
    probe family depends on fork isolation and SIGSYS survival, and a
    defect there is discovered late and cascades. Mitigated by holding the
    engine's scope fixed and small, by having the other three workstreams
    develop against the specification format rather than against the
    engine itself, and by keeping week 16 empty as declared slack. If the
    engine slips, the deferred corpus set is abandoned before any
    reduction is made to attribution, evaluation or the environment
    matrix.


13. ETHICS AND RESPONSIBLE USE

The instrument probes security boundaries, and three commitments follow.

The corpus contains no exploit code. Probes establish that a kernel entry
point is reachable and stop there; no probe performs an operation whose
success confers privilege, escapes a container, or damages the host. Where
an enumeration interface can answer the question without executing the
probed operation, it is used in preference.

The instrument is intended for infrastructure the operator owns or has
written authorisation to test. This is stated in the documentation and in
the tool's own output.

Where the instrument identifies a genuine defect in a runtime, distribution
or managed platform rather than a misconfiguration by its operator, the
finding is reported to the responsible vendor under coordinated disclosure
before publication. Publication means the public release of the instrument
and the report; the examined report is submitted regardless, and where a
vendor's window has not closed by then it describes the finding generically
and withholds the detail until it has.


14. SCOPE

Out of scope: continuous runtime monitoring and intrusion detection, which
is the province of existing tooling; generation of seccomp profiles from
application workload traces, which is well served and is not this project's
question; exploitation of the entry points the instrument measures;
userspace package vulnerability scanning; and non-Linux platforms.

The instrument measures and reports. It does not enforce, and it is not a
substitute for the enforcement mechanisms whose effect it measures.
